## Pacing and the RevPAR Index

1.  **Pacing**: is future occupancy
    
2.  If the market is booked but your property isn't then the price is too high, but if your property is getting unusual high occupancy this means maybe the rate is too low
    
3.  RevPAR(Revenue per available room)
    
4.  The RevPAR Index compares your RevPAR to the average RevPAR of your "competitive set"—the group of 4 to 6 properties nearby that your guests also consider booking.
    

*   **Index > 100:** You are "outperforming" the market. You are capturing more than your fair share of revenue.
    
*   **Index = 100:** You are performing exactly at the market average.
    
*   **Index < 100:** You are "under performing." The market is getting a better combination of rate and occupancy than you are.
    

5.  if the Index is higher than 100 it could be a real win or the price is too low to verify: compare ADR to the market and your own ADR from the same period last year if it is beaten the market and the previous ADR it is a real win, if it is less than the market or as the same as the last year: it is too cheap and needs to be updated
    

6.  as long as the stay date is far away we can higher the price but when the date approaches and the Property is not booked we need to lower the price
    
7.  Seasonal Shifts: in high seasons we prioritize the highest price that we can get and in low seasons we prioritize occupancy and reach the bare minimum that just keeps the lights on
    

**RevPAR Index Calculator**
===========================

RevPAR = ADR × Occupancy%  
RevPAR Index = (Your RevPAR ÷ Market RevPAR) × 100

## 2. Weekly listibg review

1.  a review of the pricelabs calendar of all listings in the upcoming thirty days and diagnosing any underpreforming listings or things the algorithm missed
    

### **The Workspace & Key Signals**

Your primary tool is the **PriceLabs (PL) Multi-Calendar, filtered to show the next 30 days**.

you are evaluating every listing against two specific metrics:

1.  **Last Booked Date (LBD):** How many days has it been since the listing got a booking?
    
2.  **30-Day Occupancy:** What percentage of the next 30 days is currently booked?
    

**The Decision Tree & Thresholds**

**The 40% Cutoff (The Safe Zone)** If a listing is already booked 40% or more for the next 30 days, it is performing adequately

If a listing is **below 40% occupancy**, you apply one of two thresholds:

**1\. The 7-Day Threshold (The Warning)** If it has been 7 days since the last booking:

*   Manually drop the price for the **next 7 available days** down to the "Minimum Price" established in PriceLabs.
    
*   Apply the Airbnb Promotion Protocol
    

**2\. The 15-Day Threshold (The Emergency)** If it has been 15 days since the last booking:

*   Manually drop the price for the **next 7 available days** down to the Minimum Price.
    
*   Perform a Full 5-Step Listing Audit to figure out what is fundamentally broken.
    

* * *

### **The Airbnb Promotion Protocol**

When a price drop is triggered, you don't just change the number in PriceLabs. You use Airbnb’s internal "Promotions" tool. You must follow three strict rules:

1.  **The Window:** Only apply promotions to available nights within the **next 5 days**.
    
2.  **The Price Target:** Aim for the lowest displayed price currently for those dates. _Crucially, you must separate weekday vs. weekend logic._ If weekends are naturally higher in the market, your target price should reflect that.
    
3.  **The Floor (Absolute Rule):** _Never_ set a promotion that drops the rate below the PriceLabs "Minimum Price".
    

* * *

### **The 5-Step Listing Audit (For 15-Day Emergencies)**

1.  **Compset Check:** Look at your local competitive set in PriceLabs if they're getting bookings. Compare your Average Daily Rate (ADR) and Lead Time (booking window) to theirs.
    
2.  **Length of Stay (LOS) Check: compare your LOS to Compset**
    
3.  **Airbnb Listing Quality:** Look at the live listing as a guest.
    
4.  **Confirm Fixed Discounts:** Ensure that weekly, monthly, LOS, early bird, and last-minute discounts are actively configured on Airbnb.
    
5.  **Review Score Check:** Audit the last 6 reviews. A single review of 3 stars or fewer severely damages the listing's "Quality Score" in the Airbnb algorithm
    

* * *

### **Documentation and Escalation**

### **Updating PriceLabs Notes** After reviewing a listing, you _must_ update the PL Listing Notes with:

*   Any promotions or manual overrides you applied.
    
*   Specific audit findings (e.g., "Compset is $15 cheaper").
    
*   Actions taken (e.g., "Dropped min stay from 3 to 2 nights for next 10 days").
    

**When to Escalate via HelpScout:** You must open a HelpScout ticket for the client if:

*   **The Floor Needs Moving:** You cannot get bookings even at the Minimum Price.
    
*   **Aggressive LOS Changes:** You need to drop a minimum stay requirement that the client previously insisted on keeping.
    
*   **Physical Property Issues**
    
*   **Poor Reviews:** Any review of 3 stars or less must be flagged immediately for the client
    

**The Ultimate Goal:** At the end of your weekly review Every single listing in your portfolio must be either "on track" (over 40% booked) or actively "under intervention" (price dropped or audited).

## 2- Pacing & Client Activity Cycle (Protocol 2)

**1\. Protocol 2 Overview** Protocol 2 is a biweekly diagnostic health check that looks 12 months into the future. Its purpose is to check a listing's relative position in the market by comparing its performance to competitors.

**2\. The 7 Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)** To find month-by-month trends, you track seven KPIs:

*   **RevPAR Index:** The primary signal; measures revenue per available room against the market average.
    
*   **Listing Occupancy %**
    
*   **Market Occupancy %**
    
*   **Listing ADR** (Average Daily Rate)
    
*   **Market ADR**
    
*   **Listing Occupancy Pickup (14-day):** New bookings gained in the last two weeks.
    
*   **Market Occupancy Pickup (14-day):** New bookings the market gained in the last two weeks.
    

**3\. Detection Signals** You only act when specific thresholds are crossed:

*   **Underperformance:** RevPAR Index is less than 85 **AND** Market Occupancy is 20% or higher. _(Note: If Market Occupancy is below 20%, the market is considered "dead" and no action is taken because data is unreliable)._
    
*   **Overperformance:** RevPAR Index is greater than 130. This is a warning that nights might be selling too quickly and too cheaply.
    

**4\. The 180-Day Rule** How you fix a triggered signal depends on how far away the dates are:

*   **Direct Action Zone (0 to 180 days):** You are allowed to make targeted, manual calendar overrides (moving rates up or down).
    
*   **Model Review Zone (181 to 365 days):** Manual calendar edits are strictly forbidden. Instead, you must request a model review to adjust the base price or seasonal profile.
    
    *   _Exceptions:_ Direct client request, blatant pricing error, or specific local event pricing the model missed.
        

**5\. Diagnosis and Documentation** Before changing a price, check the Neighborhood Data view to diagnose the specific nights causing the issue (e.g., a long vacant window or a specific weekend). Every manual override in the 0-180 day window must be documented in the system using the format: **\[PACE – INITIALS\]**. (eg. \[PACE - AH\] Applied gap filler discount for a long vacant window)

**6\. Override Expiry Rules** Overrides are temporary. They expire based on these rules:

*   **Rate Increases (Overperformance):** Expires after the Booking Window multiplied by 1.5 (e.g., a 30-day booking window means the override expires in 45 days).
    
*   **Rate Decreases (Underperformance):** Stays in place until your next biweekly pacing cycle, where it must be re-evaluated if still struggling we adjust the rate again or esclate the issue.
    

**7\. The Three-Month Escalation Trigger** If a listing triggers a detection signal (under or overperformance) for **three consecutive months of a problem on the calendar** (for example, seeing a signal triggered for June, July, and August). it is considered a systemic failure. You must escalate this by creating a ClickUp task assigned to the Lead Pricing Strategist containing:

**  
Listing ID and Client Name:** For immediate identification.

•**Affected Months:** Which specific months triggered the signal.

•**Escalation Reason:** A clear description of the trend you are seeing.

•**Historical Context:** How this listing performed during this time last year.

•**Occupancy vs. Market:** The specific gap in filling the calendar.

•**ADR vs. Market:** The specific gap in pricing.

•**Blocker Notes:** Any listing-level issues like poor reviews, bad photos, or restrictive house rules that might be suppressing performance regardless of price.

## 2- Diagnosis Workflow

**1\. The Role of Diagnosis** A professional Revenue Manager does not immediately drop rates when a listing is slow. Instead, they run a diagnosis workflow to justify any actions with data.

**2\. Step 1: Spotting the Signal (The Pacing Report)** The pacing report is a triage tool used to spot outliers by comparing your listing's forward booking curve to the market's curve. Pacing must always be read as a relative measurement (comparing your shape/gap to the market, not just looking at absolute percentages).

*   **On-pace:** The curve is steady and tracks with the market. No action is required.
    
*   **Lagging:** The curve is flat or slow while the market accelerates, widening the gap. This is your signal to investigate.
    

**3\. Step 2: Isolating "Nights Driving the Month"** A lagging month is usually dragged down by a handful of specific nights (like an event date or holiday). You must find these nights before changing prices using two views in Neighborhood Data:

*   **Future Occupancy:** Scan for spikes night-by-night. If the market is highly booked (e.g., 60%) on a specific Tuesday and you are empty, that night is driving the month.
    
*   **New Bookings:** Shows when the market is actively getting reservations. If you are missing out on these booking concentrations, your price or minimum stay is likely the cause.
    

**4\. Step 3: Confirming the Cause** Once the specific nights are isolated, ask these three questions in order. The first "yes" determines your action:

1.  **Are you priced above the market peak for those specific nights?** If yes, consider a targeted calendar override
    
2.  **Is your minimum stay filtering you out?** If yes, you have a length-of-stay problem that requires client approval via Helpscout.
    
3.  **Are those nights surrounded by long vacant windows?** If yes, the model’s LOS gates might be too aggressive, or your minimum price floor is blocking the algorithm.
    

**5\. The Most Common Mistake: Reacting Too Quickly**

You should slow down for three reasons:

*   **Booking curves are steep:** The market may simply not have started booking those dates yet (e.g., 60 days out).
    
*   **Need for a pattern:** A single data point is not enough; you need confirmation across neighboring nights before acting.
    
*   **Price is the last lever:** If your rate is already competitive, dropping it further only erodes your Average Daily Rate (ADR) for guests who would have booked anyway.
    

**6\. The Final Workflow Order** You must always follow this specific chain: Spot the signal (pacing report) ➔ isolate specific nights (Neighborhood Data) ➔ confirm the cause (compset) ➔ choose the action.

## 2.Events & Seasonal Profile Maintenance (Protocols 3 & 4)

**1\. The Philosophy of Calendar Management** Revenue Management requires a systematic calendar of checks so prices are high _before_ demand arrives. Protocols 3 and 4 are recurring "deep cleans" of the pricing calendar

* * *

**2\. Protocol 3: The 6-Month Events Planner** Every six months, you review the upcoming year for every property to identify high-demand, non-holiday events (like concerts or conferences).

*   **Step 1: Pulling the Data:** Use the Synchronest Local Events AI model. Input the property address and request all significant events within a 15-mile radius for the next 12 months.
    
*   **Step 2: The Human Audit:** You must filter the AI's results for:
    
    *   **Relevance:** Does the event actually bring people from out of town?
        
    *   **Exclusion:** Remove national holidays
        
    *   **Completeness:** Do a Google search to ensure the AI didn't miss anything.
        
*   **Step 3: Applying Overrides in PriceLabs:** When "pinning" dates, you must apply specific logic:
    
    *   **Markup Rule:** Apply a percentage increase based on the event's impact.
        
    *   **LOS Rules (Length of Stay):** Implement minimum stays (e.g., 3 nights for a 3-day festival)
        
    *   **Expiry Rules:** Set the overrides to expire or soften if unbooked 14 days before the event.
        
    *   **Custom Note:** Every override must include a note formatted exactly as: **"\[Event Name\] - \[Your Name\]"**.
        

* * *

**3\. Protocol 4: The Annual Seasonal Profile Update** Every December 1st, you perform a "hard reset" for the upcoming calendar year to adjust for holidays that shift dates (e.g., Thanksgiving or Easter). If possible, update at the highest level of organization (like a "Group" or "Subgroup" for homes in the same neighborhood) rather than listing-by-listing.

**The Five Steps of the Annual Update:**

1.  **Pull Existing Profiles:** Review the "Seasonality" and "Holidays" tabs in PriceLabs.
    
2.  **Flag Date Shifts:** Identify which holidays "float" and have moved dates.
    
3.  **Research New Dates:** Find the exact dates for the upcoming year's holidays.
    
4.  **Apply the Update:** Change the dates in the Seasonal Profile.
    
5.  **The Pricing Audit:** Verify the "math" using two specific benchmarks:
    
    *   **Compset 90th Percentile:** Your holiday price should be at or above the 90th percentile of your direct competitors.
        
    *   **Last-Year ADR Check:** Check what the property actually earned on that holiday last year. If your current settings are generating a lower price than last year's earnings, you must adjust the "Factor" or "Base Price" to meet or exceed it.
        

* * *

**4\. The Importance of Overlap Checks** If an event (Protocol 3) happens on the same dates as a holiday (Protocol 4), you have an "Overlap Conflict." You cannot assume the software will automatically pick the higher price. You must manually intervene and choose a "Winning" rule, which usually means combining the two markups (e.g., combining two 20% markups into a single 40% "Super-Peak" override).

## 2.  Signal-to-Action Reference

1.  this is "Action Menu" of valid moves and two strict "Gates" that must be cleared before you are authorized to pull any pricing lever.
    

**2\. The Action Menu (Your Tactical Playbook)** When reviewing a listing, you are only authorized to use one of these seven protocol-defined responses:

1.  **The Skip:** The most frequent move. You skip if occupancy is already above your target threshold, or if Market Occupancy is below 20%.
    
2.  **The Airbnb Promotion:** A short-term visibility boost for gaps in the next 5 days. You set this at the lowest displayed price, strictly separating weekday vs. weekend logic.
    
3.  **The 7-Day Markdown:** An emergency move for units significantly behind pacing targets. You drop the price for the next 7 days to the absolute minimum floor.
    
4.  **The Calendar Override:** Manual rate adjustments for pacing issues between today and 180 days out. You must leave a note using the format **\[PACE – INITIALS\]** to document the change.
    
5.  **The 5-Step Listing Audit:** If pricing is correct but the unit isn't booking, the "product" is the problem. You check the lead photo, title, description, amenity tags, and recent reviews.
    
6.  **Client Escalation (Helpscout):** Used for issues outside your control. Send a ticket to the client if you need to change the minimum price, adjust LOS restrictions, update photos, or address low review scores.
    
7.  **Lead Pricing Strategist Escalation (ClickUp):** If a listing triggers a performance alarm for three consecutive months, you escalate it via ClickUp for a "Deep Dive" strategy overhaul.
    

**3\. The First Gate: The "Dead Market" Filter** You must not react to a low RevPAR Index in isolation. The underperformance signal is a two-part lock. You only act if:

*   Your RevPAR Index is below 85.
    
*   **AND** the Market Occupancy is 20% or higher.
    

If Market Occupancy is below 20% (e.g., 12%), the market is "dead."

**4\. The Second Gate: The 180-Day Boundary** You are only authorized to make manual calendar overrides and pacing adjustments between **today and 180 days out**.

**5\. The Math Under the Signals** The numbers triggering these actions rely on two formulas:

*   **RevPAR** = Occupancy × ADR
    
*   **RevPAR Index** = (Your RevPAR / Market RevPAR) × 100
    

**6\. The Final Check** Before taking any action, always ask two questions:

1.  Is the Market Occupancy at least 20%?
    
2.  Am I looking at a date within the next 180 days? If the answer to either question is "No," your required action is **The Skip**.

## Topic 3.1 — Aggressive vs. Conservative Model Selection

  
  
**SOP: Sensitivity Model Selection**
==========================================

This SOP outlines the process for deciding whether to place a listing in the **Aggressive** or **Conservative** sensitivity model within Synchronest.

**Criteria for Selection**
--------------------------

We rely on three specific data points. We do not guess; we follow the numbers.

1.  **Review Count (Reviews≥20_Reviews_≥20):**
    
    *   **Requirement:** A listing must have at least 20 reviews.
        
    *   **Reason:** Below this, the rating is not reliable by the market.
        
2.  **Review Score (Score≥4.5_Score_≥4.5):**
    
    *   **Requirement:** The average star rating must be 4.5 or higher.
        
    *   **Reason:** Most guests filter for "4.5 and up." If the score is lower, we must compensate with conservative pricing
        
3.  **Photo Quality (Professional-Grade):**
    
    *   **Requirement:** Photos must feature professional lighting, wide angles showing the layout clearly, and thoughtful staging.
        
    *   **Reason:** High-quality photos remove the fear of a bad stay. Dark, blurry, or cell-phone photos fail this criterion.
        

**Decision Logic**
------------------

When configuring a property, evaluate the three criteria above.

*   **All Three Met:** Select the **Aggressive Model** (designed for optimization).
    
*   **Mixed Criteria (The Majority Rule):**
    
    *   If the listing **meets 2 out of the 3** requirements →→ Select the **Aggressive Model**.
        
    *   If the listing **fails 2 or more** requirements →→ Select the **Conservative Model** (designed for protection).
        
*   **Ongoing Review:** This is not a permanent decision. As a listing gains more reviews, improves its score, or receives new professional photos, this choice should be revisited to try and "graduate" the listing to the Aggressive model.

## Topic 3.2 — Base Price, Minimum Price, and Property Score

**SOP: Property Score & Base/Minimum Pricing**
==============================================

**Relevant Glossary**
---------------------

*   **Property Score:** A value from 1 to 6 that objectively compares a property to the market inventory based on its percentile rank and major amenities.
    
*   **Major Amenity:** An amenity that fundamentally changes the guest experience and price point (e.g., private pools, hot tubs, direct beach access).
    

*   **Strategy Modifier:** A final 5%5% adjustment applied to both Base and Minimum prices based on the Aggressive vs. Conservative model choice.
    

**1\. Assigning a Property Score**
----------------------------------

Evaluate the property against these criteria to anchor pricing expectations objectively:

*   **Score 1:** Budget properties with basic finishes and no notable amenities.
    
*   **Score 2:** Standard properties that are clean and functional but lack any "wow" factor.
    
*   **Score 3:** True market average. The property has modern basics and perhaps one minor upgrade.
    
*   **Score 4:** Above-average homes. Updated kitchens, better furniture, and strong professional photography.
    
*   **Score 5:** Premium properties. High-end finishes and at least one major amenity like a hot tub or a view.
    
*   **Score 6:** The luxury tier. Multiple major amenities (e.g., pool and a view) representing the best the market offers.
    

**2\. Calculating the Minimum Price**
-------------------------------------

The Minimum Price is calculated by taking the Market ADR, applying a discount based on the Property Score, and applying a final Strategy Modifier.

1.  **Identify Market ADR** for the specific bedroom count.
    
2.  **Apply Property Score Modifier:**
    
    *   Score 1: -20%
        
    *   Score 2: -15%
        
    *   Score 3: -10%
        
    *   Score 4: -5%
        
    *   Score 5: 0% (Market ADR)
        
    *   Score 6: +5%
        
    *   _Formula:_ Price\_min=ADR×(1+ScoreModifier)_Price_\__min_\=_ADR_×(1+_ScoreModifier_)
        
3.  **Apply Strategy Modifier:**
    
    *   **Aggressive:** Add 5%
        

*   **Conservative:** Subtract 5%
    

1.  **Final Safety Check:** Compare the final calculated minimum to the client's currently set minimum price. **Always choose the lower of the two numbers.**
    

**3\. Calculating the Base Price**
----------------------------------

The Base Price adjustments are more optimistic than the floor.

1.  **Identify Market ADR** for the specific bedroom count.
    
2.  **Apply Property Score Modifier:**
    
    *   Score 1: -15%
        
    *   Score 2: -10%
        
    *   Score 3: -5%
        
    *   Score 4: 0% (Market ADR)
        
    *   Score 5: +5%
        
    *   Score 6: +10%
        
    *   _Formula:_ Price\_base=ADR×(1+ScoreModifier)_Price_\__base_\=_ADR_×(1+_ScoreModifier_)
        
3.  **Apply Strategy Modifier:**
    
    *   **Aggressive:** Add 5%
        

*   **Conservative:** Subtract 5%

## Topic 3.3 — Length of Stay Tier Model

**Relevant Glossary**
---------------------

*   **Length of Stay (LOS) Tier Model:** A strategy that treats time as a variable, adjusting minimum stay requirements based on how close the check-in date is to protect far-out dates while remaining competitive in the near term.
    
*   **Median Booking Window:** A metric indicating how many days in advance the "typical" guest books their stay.
    
*   **Tier 3 (The Anchor):** The baseline or standard minimum stay desired for a specific season.
    
*   **Tier 2 (The Booking Window):** Activates inside the median booking window, slightly reducing the minimum stay to capture market demand.
    
*   **Tier 1 (The Near-Term):** Activates in the immediate future, lowering the minimum stay further to prevent empty nights.
    
*   **Tier 0 (The Last-Minute):** An optional tier for 1-night stays that triggers within 5 days of check-in.
    
*   **Tier 4 (The Far-Out Protection):** A defensive tier that activates when a high season hits >90% occupancy, raising the minimum stay to force high-value bookings.
    
*   **Orphan Gap:** A small block of empty nights sandwiched between two confirmed bookings.
    

**1\. Identifying and Organizing Seasons**
------------------------------------------

Before setting stay requirements, you must know when people are booking.

1.  Use **PriceLabs (PL) AI Insights** to identify High/Low seasons and their median booking windows.
    
2.  **Collapse Logic for Seasons:**
    
    *   If High & Low Booking Windows are within **10 days** of each other →→ Combine into one year-round profile.
        
    *   If a Shoulder season Booking Window is within **10 days** of High or Low →→ Merge it with the closest season.
        
3.  **Always record** these decisions and findings in Synchronest Notes for team transparency.
    

**2\. Tier Model "Collapse and Skip" Logic**
--------------------------------------------

The tier model is flexible. You do not always use all 5 tiers. Adjust the model based on these specific conditions:

*   **Condition:** Median LOS is < 3 nights →→ **Action:** Use 2-tier model only.
    
*   **Condition:** Client's hard minimum stay equals your Tier 3 →→ **Action:** Skip Tiers 1 and 2 (you cannot drop below the client's floor).
    
*   **Condition:** Client allows 1-night stays →→ **Action:** Add Tier 0.
    
*   **Condition:** High Season Occupancy is > 90% →→ **Action:** Add Tier 4.
    

**3\. Orphan Gap Override Rules**
---------------------------------

Even with a perfect tier model, orphan gaps will appear. These rules act as a hard "override" to the tier model to prevent unbookable slivers of time:

*   **1–2 Night Gaps:** Always allow 1-night stays to fill these gaps (regardless of the current tier).
    
*   **3–5 Night Gaps:** Reduce the minimum stay to exactly match the gap size (e.g., a 4-night gap gets a 4-night minimum). This prevents a guest from booking 2 nights and creating two new 1-night orphans.

## Topic 3.4 — Discount Structures (last-minute, gap, adjacent, far-out)

**SOP: Discount & Premium Structures**
======================================

**Relevant Glossary**
---------------------

*   **Conservative Strategy (Discounts):** Takes fewer risks with empty nights by offering deeper discounts to ensure the property fills up (protects occupancy). Best for high-volume, thin-margin properties.
    
*   **Aggressive Strategy (Discounts):** Holds out for higher-paying guests with smaller discounts, even if it risks empty nights (protects price). Best for luxury "trophy" properties.
    

*   **Adjacent Stay:** A stay that starts or ends within 3 days of an existing reservation.
    
*   **Far-Out Premium:** A "convenience fee" charged for booking far in advance, as the guest is buying certainty and locking up inventory early.
    

**1\. Last-Minute Discounts**
-----------------------------

This addresses perishable inventory. Once a night passes, its value is zero.

*   **Trigger Window:** 15 days before check-in.
    
*   **Execution:** The discount must be _gradual_ to prevent a sudden pricing cliff.
    
*   **Conservative Model:** 20% gradual discount.
    
*   **Aggressive Model:** 15% gradual discount.
    

**2\. Gap Night Discounts**
---------------------------

This targets 1 to 2-day orphans between existing reservations. Larger gaps (3+ days) are left to the standard pricing engine.

*   **Condition:** In markets with high weekend occupancy, restrict these discounts to weekdays only. There is no reason to discount a Saturday if it will book at full price.
    
*   **Conservative Model:** 10% discount.
    
*   **Aggressive Model:** 5% discount.
    

**3\. Adjacent Stay Discounts**
-------------------------------

This rewards guests for creating back-to-back stays, which reduces empty time and creates a tight, predictable schedule for cleaning crews.

*   **Trigger Window:** A stay that starts or ends within 3 days of an existing reservation.
    
*   **Condition:** Like gap nights, typically restricted to weekdays to "stretch" existing weekend bookings into the week.
    
*   **Conservative Model:** 10% discount.
    
*   **Aggressive Model:** 5% discount.
    

**4\. Far-Out Pricing Adjustments (Premiums)**
----------------------------------------------

This is a premium, not a discount. Guests booking far in advance are buying certainty. This protects the owner from underpricing dates that may turn out to be major events.

*   **Trigger Window:** Begins at 90 days in advance and scales over 180 days.
    
*   **Conservative Model:** 10% gradual premium.
    
*   **Aggressive Model:** 20% gradual premium.
    
*   **Formula:** Price=Base×(1+Premium)_Price_\=_Base_×(1+_Premium_)

## Topic 3.5 — Fees, Cancellation Policy, Events, and Holiday Profiles

**Relevant Glossary**
---------------------

*   **The Rounding Rule:** The practice of always rounding cleaning and extra guest fees to the nearest $5.
    
*   **Sticky Booking:** A booking that is highly unlikely to cancel, secured by offering a 10% discount in exchange for a non-refundable policy.
    
*   **Expiry Day (Events):** The date close to a major event where you drop the aggressive markup to prevent an empty house.
    
*   **Occupancy Delta (Holidays):** The mathematical difference between how full the market is during a holiday compared to the normal high season.
    
*   **Competitor Set (Comps):** A strictly filtered list of properties used to baseline your data.
    

**1\. Cleaning & Guest Fees**
-----------------------------

Fees are the first thing a guest sees. If they are too high, they kill the booking at checkout.

1.  Find the local market range in the PriceLabs "Neighborhood Data" or "Competitor" tabs.
    
2.  Pick a number on the _lower end_ of that range to remain competitive.
    
3.  Apply the **Rounding Rule**: Round down to the nearest $5.
    

**2\. Day-of-Week Adjustments**
-------------------------------

Do not manually guess weekend markups. Use these automated occupancy tiers to move with the momentum of the market:

*   ≤30%≤30% full: **\-15%**
    
*   ≤40%≤40% full: **\-10%**
    
*   ≤50%≤50% full: **\-5%**
    
*   ≤60%≤60% full: **+5%**
    
*   ≤70%≤70% full: **+10%**
    
*   ≤80%≤80% full: **+15%**
    

**3\. Cancellations & Airbnb Discounts**
----------------------------------------

*   **Cancellations:** Never move to a stricter policy than the market average. _Always_ include a Non-Refundable option for a 10% discount.
    
*   **Standard Airbnb Discounts:** Use these tested values for almost every property:
    
    *   **Early Bird:** 5%
        
    *   **Last-Minute (within 7 days):** 5%
        
    *   **Weekly:** 11%
        
    *   **Monthly:** 18%
        
    *   **Length of Stay (LOS):** Sliding scale starting at 3% (2 nights) up to 7% (6 nights).
        

**4\. Local Event Markups**
---------------------------

Use a GPT prompt to scrape local calendars for events with >50,000 attendees within a 20km radius. Use the Event Markup Grid to balance attendee size and distance. _Crucial:_ Always set an **Expiry Day** (e.g., 3 days out) to drop the markup if it hasn't booked.

**5\. Holiday Markups (The Delta)**
-----------------------------------

Holidays are seasons, not spikes. Calculate the Delta (`Holiday Occupancy %` minus `High Season Occupancy %`) to find your markup:

*   Delta is ±5%±5%: **10% markup**
    
*   Delta > 5%: **15% markup**
    
*   Delta > 10%: **20% markup**
    
*   Delta > 15%: **25% markup**
    
*   Delta > 20%: **30% markup**
    

**6\. Defining the Competitor Set**
-----------------------------------

A comp is only valid if it meets ALL four criteria:

1.  **Quality:** ≥10≥10 reviews and a minimum score of 4.7.
    
2.  **Size:** Same bedroom count _and_ similar guest capacity.
    
3.  **Amenities:** Must match "large" amenities (e.g., pools, hot tubs).
    
4.  **Proximity:** Must be within 5 km.

## Topic 4.2 — Compset, Channel Rules, and Launch Audits

**Compset Building and Launch Audits**
======================================

This SOP details how to build a compliant competitive set and the auditing procedures required before and after launching dynamic pricing.

**Related Glossary Terms**
--------------------------

*   **Compliant Compset:** A filtered group of 10 to 15 listings PriceLabs looks at to determine market trends, removing any "dirty data".
    
*   **Standard Criteria (Compset):** Listings within a 5-kilometer radius, same bedroom count, min 10 reviews, and min 4.7 rating.
    
*   **Dirty Data (Compset):** Listings that skew data, including those with N/A prices, greyed-out calendars, fixed prices, or out-of-range LOS.
    
*   **Markup:** Adjusting rates higher to cover OTA host fees and ensure take-home pay remains consistent.
    
*   **Smart Pricing (Airbnb):** An Airbnb feature that pushes rates lower to increase booking fees; must be disabled.
    
*   **Rule Sets (Airbnb):** Native Airbnb pricing rules that conflict with PriceLabs; must be removed.
    

**Step 6: Building a Compliant Compset**
----------------------------------------

The goal is to filter out "noise" so PriceLabs only looks at true rivals (aiming for 10-15 high-quality competitors).

1.  **Apply Standard Criteria:**
    
    *   Within a 5-kilometer radius (expand slightly if needed, but never compromise on size/quality).
        
    *   Same bedroom count.
        
    *   Minimum 10 reviews.
        
    *   Minimum 4.7 rating.
        
2.  **Filter Out "Dirty Data":** Remove listings exhibiting the following:
    
    *   **N/A Prices or Greyed Out Calendars:** Likely off-market or long-term rentals.
        
    *   **Fixed Prices:** Same price every day for 6 months (they aren't using dynamic pricing and will anchor the market).
        
    *   **LOS Out of Range:** e.g., A 30-day minimum stay competitor when you are a short-term rental.
        

**Step 7: Channel Rules (PMS vs. OTA)**
---------------------------------------

Establish a strict hierarchy to keep data clean and avoid unpredictable results.

1.  **The PMS (Source of Truth):**
    
    *   Apply cleaning fees, cancellation policies, and long-stay discounts (weekly/monthly) directly in the PMS.
        
2.  **The OTA:**
    
    *   Use OTAs ONLY for channel-specific marketing (e.g., "New Listing" 20% discount on Airbnb only).
        
3.  **Applying Markups:**
    
    *   Markups offset OTA host fees to keep net income consistent. Formula: `TotalPrice = BasePrice / (1 - CommissionPercentage)`
        
    *   If a PMS is used, apply channel markups directly in the PMS.
        
    *   If no PMS is used, apply markups in PriceLabs under the "pricing offset" section.
        

**Step 8: The Pre-Launch Audit**
--------------------------------

Before hitting "Sync", run this safety check to prevent costly errors.

1.  **Disable OTA Overrides (Airbnb):**
    
    *   Disable "Smart Pricing".
        
    *   Remove all "Rule Sets" in the hosting dashboard.
        
2.  **Pre-Launch Checklist:**
    
    *   **Group Assignment:** Is the listing in the correct PriceLabs group?
        
    *   **Floor and Ceiling Prices:** Does the minimum cover costs? Does the maximum capture peak demand?
        
    *   **Minimum Stay:** Are rules accurate for weekdays and weekends?
        
    *   **Legacy Strategies:** Have all old rate strategies/manual overrides in the PMS been removed?
        
    *   **Historical Review:** Does the new price look reasonable compared to last year's booked rates?
        

**Step 9: Post-Launch and Expert Settings**
-------------------------------------------

After syncing, verify that the data is flowing correctly.

1.  **Side-by-Side Verification:** Open the PMS calendar and the Airbnb/VRBO calendars simultaneously.
    
    *   Check that prices match (factoring in the applied markup).
        
    *   Verify cleaning fees and minimum stays carried over.
        
    *   If dates are unexpectedly "blocked," check PMS settings first.
        
2.  **Partner Settings:**
    
    *   Add `pricelabs@synchronest.com` as an admin to every client account so the strategy team can monitor performance.

**Listing Audit / Conversion Engine**
=====================================

This SOP outlines the standards for auditing a listing to fix the "leaky bucket"—when a property gets high search impressions but fails to convert those into bookings.

**THE OVERRIDING RULE:** If a client uses a Property Management System (PMS), ALL changes (description, amenities, etc.) must be made in the PMS, not on Airbnb directly. Changes made on Airbnb will be overwritten during the next PMS sync.

**1\. The Header Photo (The Stop-the-Scroll Test)**
---------------------------------------------------

The header photo's only job is to prove the property is different or better than competitors to earn the click.

*   **Look for a USP:** Ensure it highlights a "Wow Factor" (e.g., a view, a pool) rather than a generic shot (e.g., folded towels).

------------------------------------------

These photos act as a "highlight reel," not a chronological tour. They dictate if a guest keeps reading.

*   Each photo should show a distinct, high-value space (e.g., Living Room, Primary Bedroom, Deck).
    
*   They should build an emotional pull (lifestyle shots).
    
*   Each should be strong enough to act as a header photo on its own.
    

**3\. Overall Photo Quality and the Missing Spaces Check**
----------------------------------------------------------

*   **Technical Quality:** Flag dark, poorly lit, or badly angled photos for a reshoot.
    
*   **Missing Spaces Check:** Cross-reference listing claims with visual evidence. Guests assume unseen areas don't exist or are in poor condition.
    
    *   Does every claimed bedroom have at least two photos from different angles?
        
    *   Is every bathroom shown?
        
    *   Is there a clear shot of the entrance or parking?
        

**4\. Photo Tour and Photo Tags (Airbnb specific)**
---------------------------------------------------

*   **Photo Tour:** Ensure photos are assigned to the correct room.
    
*   **Photo Tags:** Ensure specific amenities are tagged in photos (e.g., "Chef's Kitchen") to help the algorithm and provide context to skimming guests. Mix wide-angle shots with tight detail shots.

------------------------------------------------

Describe the _experience_, not just the house.

*   **Headline:** Maximize the character limit. Lead with the strongest selling point in the first 3–5 words. Avoid subjective filler words ("Beautiful", "Cozy"). Use symbols (`|` or `+`) to cram in keywords.
    
*   **Description:**
    
    *   Avoid the "Wall of Text"—use headers and bullets for mobile readability.
        
    *   Avoid house branding/names unless the client has a matching direct-booking site.
        
    *   Include sections for: space overview, experience highlights, nearby attractions, and host style.
        

**6\. The Amenities Audit (The 40-Amenity Threshold)**
------------------------------------------------------

Amenities dictate visibility in search filters.

*   **The 40-Amenity Threshold:** If fewer than 40 amenities are checked, flag it. It is likely missing basic items.
    
*   **Cross-Reference:** Ensure checked big amenities (pools, views) are visible in the photos and their status (private vs. shared) is clear.
    
*   **Check Basics:** Ensure basic amenities like Hangers, Iron, and Dedicated Workspace are not overlooked.

## Topic 5.1 — Structural Pattern Confirmation

**Model Diagnosis SOP: Step 1 - The Two-Month Rule**
====================================================

**Overview**
------------

This SOP outlines the initial step of the Model Diagnosis process. The goal is to separate signal from noise and prevent the team from making permanent model changes to address temporary market fluctuations. Before touching base rates or seasonality curves, you must prove the problem is structural using time and consistency.

**Prerequisites: The Escalation Trigger**
-----------------------------------------

Do not run a full model diagnosis for every minor dip in performance. Start this process ONLY when an escalation trigger is met.

*   **Trigger:** A property has been flagged for significant performance deviations in either the **0–180 day window** or the **181–365 day window**.
    
*   When triggered, the case is assigned to the Lead Revenue Manager.
    
*   **Goal:** Determine if the pricing model needs a fundamental adjustment, or if the Revenue Manager just needs to be more aggressive with manual calendar overrides.
    

**Step-by-Step Procedure**
--------------------------

### **1\. Check the Two-Month Rule**

You must confirm two consecutive months with a consistent deviation before considering any model change.

1.  Review the property's performance for the first flagged month (e.g., June).
    
2.  Review the performance for the immediate following month (e.g., July).
    
3.  Calculate the Deviation % for both months:
    
        
        Deviation % = ((Actual Revenue - Forecasted Revenue) / Forecasted Revenue) * 100
    
4.  **Decision Point:**
    
    *   If the deviation is NOT consistently in the same direction (e.g., negative in June, positive in July), **STOP**. Do not change the model for a bouncing property.
        
    *   If the deviation is consistently negative or positive for two months in a row, proceed to identifying the deviation type.
        

### **2\. Identify the Deviation Type**

It is not enough for two months to be bad; they must be bad for the same reason. Categorize each month into one of three deviation types:

*   **Occupancy-Driven:** Model hits price targets (ADR) but rooms are empty. (Model is likely too expensive).
    
*   **ADR-Driven:** Property is full but the achieved price is much lower than forecasted. (Model is reacting too aggressively to fill rooms, leaving money on the table).
    
*   **Mixed:** Both occupancy and ADR significantly miss the forecast. (Broader breakdown in market perception).
    

### **3\. Confirm the Structural Pattern**

1.  Compare the deviation type of Month 1 to the deviation type of Month 2.
    
2.  **Decision Point:**
    
    *   **Mismatch (e.g., Month 1 is Occupancy-Driven, Month 2 is ADR-Driven):** **STOP**. The market is volatile, and changing model rules will likely lead to "over-fitting" and worse swings. **Revert to Calendar Management.** (The Revenue Manager handles issues manually on the calendar).
        
    *   **Match (e.g., Month 1 is ADR-Driven, Month 2 is ADR-Driven):** A structural pattern is confirmed. **Proceed to Step 2** of the Model Diagnosis SOP (Identifying the primary model category).
        

**Related Glossary Terms**
--------------------------

*   **Model Diagnosis SOP:** A diagnostic process designed to separate signal from noise when evaluating model performance, ensuring that fundamental adjustments are only made to address structural problems (proven by time and consistency), rather than temporary market fluctuations.
    
*   **Escalation Trigger:** The specific condition that initiates a formal Model Diagnosis process. This typically occurs when a property is flagged for significant performance deviations in either the 0–180 day window or the 181–365 day window.
    
*   **Deviation %:** The metric used to compare actual performance against what the pricing model predicted. Calculated as: `((Actual Revenue - Forecasted Revenue) / Forecasted Revenue) * 100`.
    
*   **Occupancy-Driven Deviation:** A deviation type where the model achieves the target price (ADR) but fails to secure bookings (low occupancy), indicating the model is likely pricing too high for current market demand.
    
*   **ADR-Driven Deviation:** A deviation type where the property is full (high occupancy) but the achieved price is significantly lower than forecasted, suggesting the model is discounting too aggressively and leaving money on the table.
    
*   **Mixed Deviation:** A deviation type where both occupancy and ADR miss the forecast significantly, pointing to a broader breakdown in the model's perception of the market.
    
*   **Calendar Management:** The practice of handling pricing issues manually via calendar overrides rather than altering the underlying automated model logic, typically used when market conditions are volatile or unpredictable.
    
*   **Over-fitting (Pricing Model):** The mistake of changing the fundamental rules of a pricing model in response to chaotic or inconsistent market swings, which usually exacerbates performance issues.
    
*   **Structural Pattern:** Confirmed when a property experiences a consistent deviation in performance (e.g., negative deviation) of the exact same type (e.g., Occupancy-Driven) for two consecutive months.

## Topic 5.2 — Model Categories A–F

**Model Diagnosis SOP: Step 2 - The Six Model Categories**
==========================================================

**Related Glossary Terms**
--------------------------

*   **Reactive Management:** The practice of manually dropping prices for specific dates when a property isn't performing, which treats the symptom (empty dates) but ignores the underlying structural disease in the pricing model.
    
*   **Six Model Categories:** A classification system used during the Model Diagnosis SOP to isolate structural flaws in a pricing model into specific, actionable buckets.
    
*   **Category A (Base Price Misalignment):** A structural issue where a property is systematically over-priced or under-priced across an extended period (e.g., 3-6 months), regardless of season or local events.
    
*   **Category B (Seasonality Pricing Misalignment):** A structural issue where the model understands the market but gets the intensity of the seasons wrong (either too aggressive or too soft during peak/shoulder seasons).
    
*   **Category C (Demand Sensitivity Misalignment):** A structural issue where the model's reaction to market demand is incorrect (e.g., remaining flat when market occupancy is high, or jumping too aggressively).
    
*   **Category D (Far-Out Premium Issue):** A structural issue concerning the relationship between time-to-stay and price, typically for dates 60-90+ days away. (e.g., underpriced premiums leading to early sellouts, or overpriced premiums leading to a lack of early interest).
    
*   **Category E (Length of Stay/LOS and Restrictions):** A structural issue where the price is correct, but booking rules (like minimum stay requirements) do not match the market's demand pattern, creating dead zones.
    
*   **Category F (Non-Pricing Issue):** Issues that exist outside the model's logic causing low performance despite a competitive price, such as bad reviews, poor photos, or nearby construction.
    
*   **The One-Category-Only Rule:** A scientific rule in the Model Diagnosis SOP dictating that an escalation must be assigned to exactly one primary category to ensure clean data and surgical model adjustments.
    
*   **Noise (Market):** A temporary fluctuation in the market, a random slow week, or a localized event. Structural changes to the model should never be made based on noise.
    

* * *

**Overview**
------------

Once a structural pattern is confirmed (Step 1), you must categorize the issue into one of six specific buckets. The goal of this classification is isolation, ensuring that any adjustment made is surgical rather than a reactive "over-tweak" that makes the data noisier.

**Step-by-Step Procedure**
--------------------------

### **1\. Evaluate Against the Six Categories**

Analyze the data to determine the primary driver of the problem:

*   **Category A: Base Price Misalignment**
    
    *   **Diagnosis:** Consistent trend of low occupancy across the next 3 to 6 months, even during normal demand periods.
        
    *   **Indicator:** Consistently the most expensive listing in the competitive set without the amenities to justify it.
        
*   **Category B: Seasonality Pricing Misalignment**
    
    *   **Diagnosis:** The model gets the intensity of the seasons wrong.
        
    *   **Indicator:** Occupancy is great in the shoulder season but terrible in the peak season (aggressive profile), or booking out instantly during peak season and leaving money on the table (soft profile).
        
*   **Category C: Demand Sensitivity Misalignment**
    
    *   **Diagnosis:** The model reacts incorrectly to market demand.
        
    *   **Indicator:** The market is 90% occupied but the price is still at the base rate (too flat), or the price jumps by 50% immediately when demand picks up and kills momentum (too aggressive).
        
*   **Category D: Far-Out Premium Issue**
    
    *   **Diagnosis:** Issue with the relationship between time-to-stay and price for dates >60 or >90 days away.
        
    *   **Indicator:** "Selling the house" too early (e.g., 50% booked 6 months out) meaning premiums are too low, OR far-out prices are so high there is no early interest, leading to panic drops as the date approaches.
        
*   **Category E: Length of Stay (LOS) and Restrictions**
    
    *   **Diagnosis:** The price is perfect, but the rules are wrong. The demand pattern of the market does not match the model's restrictions.
        
    *   **Indicator:** A 5-night minimum in a market where the average visitor stays 2 nights, creating "dead zones" in the calendar.
        
*   **Category F: Non-Pricing Issue**
    
    *   **Diagnosis:** The issue exists outside the model's logic.
        
    *   **Indicator:** People are clicking the listing but not booking, and the price is competitive. Examples include a string of bad reviews, blurry photos, a newly renovated competitor, or nearby construction.
        

### **2\. Apply The One-Category-Only Rule**

*   You must choose **exactly one** category as the primary driver.
    
*   **Why:** This ensures clean data. If multiple variables are changed simultaneously, you cannot track which change fixed the problem or which change broke it further.
    
*   If you suspect multiple categories (e.g., A and B), analyze the data closer to isolate the real culprit (e.g., is underperformance happening all year (A) or just in the winter (B)?).
    

### **3\. When to Walk Away (The Noise Filter)**

*   If you evaluate the data and the issue does not cleanly fit into Categories A through F (e.g., occupancy is a bit low, but price is right, reviews are good, seasonality matches, restrictions are standard).
    
*   **Action:** **Do not make a structural change.**
    
*   The current performance dip is considered "noise" (a temporary fluctuation).
    
*   Either wait for more data or take a manual, one-off action (like a temporary discount) that does not alter the underlying model structure.

## Topic 5.3 — Lever Adjustment Guidelines and Client Approval Rules

**Model Diagnosis SOP: Step 3 - Levers, Ranges, and Approvals**
===============================================================

**Related Glossary Terms**
--------------------------

*   **Single Dominant Lever:** The specific variable in a pricing model that moves the needle without causing collateral damage; the one rule changed during a model diagnosis.
    
*   **Base Price (Lever):** The starting point for all calculations in the pricing model.
    
*   **Seasonal Base (Lever):** Adjustments made specifically for high or low seasons.
    
*   **Demand Sensitivity Tier (Lever):** A setting dictating how aggressively the model reacts to high or low demand in the market.
    
*   **Minimum Price Floor (Lever):** The absolute lowest the price can go, acting as the client's safety net.
    
*   **Length of Stay (LOS) Rules (Lever):** Requirements for how many nights a guest must book, directly affecting client operations like cleaning schedules.
    
*   **Volatility (Pricing):** When a model swings wildly from one extreme to another, usually caused by adjustments made outside defined safety ranges.
    
*   **Internal Adjustments:** Changes to the underlying pricing logic that are considered "expert calibrations" and do not require client approval (e.g., base price adjustments within 3-10%, seasonal profile shifts, moving sensitivity by one tier).
    
*   **Helpscout Escalation:** The process of obtaining written approval from the client via Helpscout for specific changes (Minimum Price Floor and LOS Rules).
    

* * *

**Overview**
------------

Once you have categorized the problem (Step 2), true model diagnosis is about finding the single variable (lever) that moves the needle without causing collateral damage. This requires a disciplined approach to identifying the dominant lever, staying within controlled ranges to prevent volatility, and knowing exactly when you need the client's permission to proceed.

**Step-by-Step Procedure**
--------------------------

### **1\. Identify the Single Dominant Lever**

Find one dominant issue and solve it with one dominant lever.

*   **Tool:** Use the **calendar hover tool** on the flagged dates to see the breakdown of how the final price was calculated.
    
*   **Goal:** Isolate the specific variable pushing the price too high or too low.
    
*   **Rule:** If weekends are too high but weekdays are fine, adjust the weekend markup or the demand sensitivity tier, NOT the base price. Adjusting the base price would break the healthy weekdays.
    

### **2\. Adjust Within Controlled Ranges**

Make incremental changes, observe the market response, and adjust again if needed to prevent volatility.

*   **Base Price and Seasonal Base:** Adjustments must generally fall between **3% and 10%**. (If a change larger than 10% is needed, the model category likely needs reassessment by the Lead RM). If a diagnosis suggests a 15% cut, start with a 10% adjustment.
    
*   **Sensitivity Tiers (Demand or Seasonal):** Limited to a shift of **exactly one tier level** (e.g., from Tier 3 to Tier 2). You cannot jump multiple tiers.
    
*   **Minimum Price and LOS:** Adjustments should be very conservative, as these levers directly block bookings.
    

### **3\. Client Approval Boundary**

Understand the hard line between internal logic and client business rules.

*   **Internal Adjustments (No Approval Required):**
    
    *   Base price adjustments within the 3-10% range.
        
    *   Seasonal profile shifts.
        
    *   Moving sensitivity tiers by one level.
        
    *   _These are expert calibrations. Asking permission for these creates friction._
        
*   **Client Approval Required (Helpscout Escalation):**
    
    *   **Minimum Price Floor:** Because it is the client's safety net (lowering involves risk; raising blocks bookings).
        
    *   **Length of Stay (LOS) Rules:** Because they affect client operations (cleaning schedules, turnover frequency).
        
    *   _When escalating, you must provide a professional recommendation including: the current strategy, proposed change, reason for the change, and expected outcome._
        

### **4\. Documenting the Escalation**

Every adjustment must be documented with precision to build a history of expertise and allow the Lead Pricing Strategist to review the changes. A proper documentation entry MUST include:

1.  **Affected Months:** Which specific part of the calendar was the focus?
    
2.  **Model Category:** Describe the specific part of the strategy that needed revisions.
    
3.  **Lever Adjusted:** Which specific variable did you change?
    
4.  **Magnitude:** How much did you change it?
    

**Example of an SOP-compliant entry:**

> "October and November (Category B); Adjusted Demand Sensitivity from Tier 3 to Tier 2 to increase volume during low-occupancy periods."
